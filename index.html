EPIDEMIOLOGICAL REPORT • ADULT DENTAL LONGEVITY

How Many Permanent Teeth Will You Retain to Age 80?

This study investigates individual tooth survival and periodontal breakdown using epidemiological data from the US CDC / NCHS National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES).
While young adults typically retain a full set of 28 permanent teeth, median dentition declines sharply after age 40. By examining tooth-by-tooth survival alongside daily interdental cleaning and socioeconomic barriers, this report clarifies the primary drivers of adult tooth loss and outlines evidence-based preventive priorities.

6. Analytical Synthesis & Recommended Actions

What the data tells us

  • First and second molars experience the highest rate of loss across all age groups.
  • The steepest decline in remaining teeth begins between ages 40 and 55.
  • Daily interdental flossing associates with an average of +6.2 more teeth at age 70.
  • Low-income cohorts visit clinics predominantly for emergency pain relief (57.8%) rather than prevention.
→ Posterior teeth succumb early due to high occlusal wear and deep furcation pockets.
→ Tooth loss is primarily driven by accumulated untreated periodontitis rather than normal biological aging.

Where data is missing (Limitations)

  • NHANES is a cross-sectional population sample rather than a decades-long continuous single-patient tracking study.
  • Specific reasons for each tooth extraction (severe caries vs periodontal bone loss vs orthodontic) are not recorded for past missing teeth.
  • Dietary sugar frequency and lifetime smoking duration require deeper longitudinal cross-matching.
→ Longitudinal patient follow-up is needed to quantify individual rate of attachment loss progression over 20-year horizons.

Data to collect next to identify root causes

  • Collect longitudinal interproximal probing depth records alongside microbiological subgingival plaque samples.
  • Track clinic attendance frequency at the 6-month recall interval to evaluate preventive maintenance efficacy.
  • Examine insurance and out-of-pocket coverage thresholds across diverse municipal health schemes.

Summary

  • Adult tooth loss is not inevitable; the age-40 divergence can be mitigated by consistent interdental hygiene.
  • Preventive visits must be prioritized before symptoms appear, particularly focusing on first and second molars.
  • Policy interventions should expand subsidized preventive cleanings to close the 38.5% access gap between income tiers.
